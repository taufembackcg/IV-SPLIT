\paragraph{Reading the pilot.} Six patterns stand out.

\emph{(i) The identification layer separates S1 from S3.} With $\lambda=1$
(Table~\ref{tab:pilot-lam1}), Cumulant-ICA with labelling (L1) is essentially median-unbiased in S1.
Its RMSE falls from $0.30$ at $n=1000$ to $0.07$ at $n=10000$, coverage is close to nominal, and the
share of replications flagged as not identified falls from $64\%$ to $0\%$. In the Gaussian S3 the
same estimator is erratic (RMSE in the units or larger), but $D$ flags $100\%$ of the replications.
This is the first test of the research plan, and the layer passes it.

\emph{(ii) Identification through higher moments is weak.} With $\lambda=0.5$
(Table~\ref{tab:pilot-fast}), $\det M\propto\lambda^2$ is four times smaller. At $n=1000$ almost
every S1 replication is flagged, and at $n=5000$ a third still are. This matches the sample sizes
that \citet{delbianco2026} needs for power. Without the $D$ filter, the RMSE of Cumulant-ICA is
dominated by a few explosive replications in which $\widehat{\det M}\approx0$.

\emph{(iii) Labelling matters, and (L2) works only where its premise holds.} In the Aux design
(L2) is median-unbiased with RMSE $0.10$ at $n=5000$. In S1, where $\Var(e_X\mid w_1)$ is constant,
the auxiliary slope is uninformative, the rule picks the wrong root about half the time, and the
median bias is close to that of OLS. Lewbel's estimator, which relies on the same
heteroskedasticity, is excellent in the Aux design and uninformative elsewhere.

\emph{(iv) Each competitor wins only in its own setting.} SP removes the bias almost completely in
S2 (median bias $0.004$ at $n=5000$), where the confounding is a quadratic in $x$, and coincides
with OLS whenever the confounding is linear. In S2 the ``oracle'' row is not a ceiling: there $e_X$
is itself the confounded variable, which shows that an instrument is only valid relative to a
model. The non-Gaussian estimators are misspecified in S2. $D$ flags about $90\%$ of replications,
and the median bias of Cumulant-ICA there exceeds $1.6$ at $n=5000$. This calls for the agreement tests of
Section~\ref{sec:tests} as a second line of defence.

\emph{(v) Violations of independence (S4) bias every internal-instrument method,} including the
oracle. Cumulant-ICA is mostly flagged. Across all replications its median bias is small for $\rho=0.3$ and
about $0.28$ (against $0.40$ for OLS) when $e_X$ has a direct effect on $u$. This motivates the sensitivity analysis of
\citet{conley2012}.

\emph{(vi) The unanchored neural split does not identify $\beta$} (Table~\ref{tab:pilot-nn}). The IV
version stays within $0.02$ of OLS in all three designs, and the control-function version is noisier
without being better, as Proposition~\ref{prop:nosplit} predicts. Encouragingly, the procedure
reports its own failure: $96$--$100\%$ of replications are flagged as not identified. The anchored
split of Section~\ref{sec:anchored} is the natural next step.
